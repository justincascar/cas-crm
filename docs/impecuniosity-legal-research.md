## What "impecuniosity" means

A claimant is "impecunious" if they could not have paid for a replacement vehicle (or repairs) without making unreasonable sacrifices. If impecuniosity is proved, the claimant can recover credit hire charges even where a cheaper "basic hire rate" (BHR) was available, because they genuinely couldn't access the cheaper option. The test comes from **Lagden v O'Connor [2003] UKHL 64; [2004] 1 AC 1067** (House of Lords) — a deliberately open-ended test, not a fixed checklist.

## Burden and structure of proof

- The legal burden is on the claimant to plead and prove impecuniosity — it does not fall to the defendant/insurer to disprove it.
- Once the claimant produces some evidence of impecuniosity, an evidential burden shifts to the defendant to challenge it.
- Confirmed by the Court of Appeal in **Zurich Insurance plc v Umerji [2014] EWCA Civ 357**, which also held that impecuniosity affects not just the rate claimed but the length of the hire period — and that a claimant debarred from pleading impecuniosity (for non-disclosure) cannot later rely on financial hardship to justify delay in repairing or replacing the vehicle either.

## Evidence actually required

- Bank statements covering the relevant period (all accounts the client holds, not just the main one)
- Wage slips or benefits award schedules
- Evidence of regular household expenditure/commitments
- An explanation for any other income or support (savings, family help, joint accounts)

Disclosure must be complete. Courts draw adverse inferences from missing statements, inconsistent date ranges, undisclosed accounts, or unexplained transfers.

- In **Umerji**, failure to disclose financial means led to the claimant being barred from pleading impecuniosity at all.
- In **Braciforte v Williams [2026] EWCC 6** (County Court at Liverpool, on appeal), a claimant who had failed to disclose a foreign (Brazilian) bank account was not debarred — the court treated it as a minor procedural breach, accepted her explanation that the account had become inaccessible, and upheld the impecuniosity finding (hire damages of £15,175.80 awarded). Courts retain discretion, but non-disclosure is a real and live risk each time.
- A solicitors' commentary (Trethowans) describes a claimant asserting impecuniosity on a £15,000+ hire claim while declaring income of £2,000–£3,000/month; closer review of bank records showed undisclosed transfers and actual earnings nearer £9,000/month — the claim was substantially reduced once challenged.

## Key case law (chronological)

| Case | Citation | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Mattocks v Mann | [1993] RTR 13 | Early recognition that impecunious claimants can recover credit hire charges |
| Dimond v Lovell | [2000] 2 AC 384 | Distinguished the basic hire element from "additional" credit-hire services |
| Clark v Ardington Electrical Services | [2002] EWCA Civ 510 | Claimants acting reasonably in instructing a reputable credit hire organisation |
| Lagden v O'Connor | [2003] UKHL 64; [2004] 1 AC 1067 | Establishes the impecuniosity/"unreasonable sacrifice" test itself |
| Bent v Highways & Utilities Construction | [2010] EWCA Civ 292 | Courts are familiar with, and can make, comparable BHR adjustments |
| Zurich Insurance plc v Umerji | [2014] EWCA Civ 357 | Burden of proof; impecuniosity goes to rate *and* period; effect of being debarred |
| Stevens v Equity Syndicate Management Ltd | [2015] EWCA Civ 93 | Criteria for an appropriate basic hire rate comparison |
| McBride v UK Insurance; Clayton v EUI | [2017] EWCA Civ 144 | Courts should not apply an unduly exacting standard to BHR evidence |
| Bunting v Zurich | [2020] EWHC 1807 (QB) | Material flaws needed before a BHR comparison can be rejected |
| Braciforte v Williams | [2026] EWCC 6 | Non-disclosure of a foreign account treated as curable procedural breach, not an automatic debar |

Case names and citations above come from practitioner commentary and the National Archives case law database, not from independently checking each full judgment. Before relying on any of these in a live dispute, pull the actual judgment from a primary source (caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk or BAILII) and verify it.
